Apple AirPods Max: Surface CAD & Photorealistic Visualization

A comprehensive Class A surfacing and visualization project reverse engineering the flagship Apple AirPods Max. The project focused on building a high precision digital surface model from scratch in Autodesk Alias, achieving strict G2 curvature continuity across organic compound transitions, complemented by high fidelity raytraced studio renders and realistic material exploration in Autodesk VRED.

Class A Surface Modeling in Autodesk Alias

To accurately capture the refined design language of Apple, the complete geometry, from the organic anodized aluminum ear cups and stainless steel telescoping arms to the tensioned knit mesh headband canopy, was constructed using advanced surface modeling techniques in Autodesk Alias. Prioritizing single span surfaces wherever feasible, the model maintains flawless G2 curvature continuity across all character lines, chamfers, and transition fillets.

Surface quality was rigorously validated using diagnostic evaluation tools including zebra stripe shading, Gaussian curvature analysis, and dynamic highlight simulation to guarantee production level surface tangency without pinching, inflection flaws, or aesthetic distortion.

Photorealistic Raytracing and Visualization in Autodesk VRED

Following surface completion, the native Alias wireframes and trimmed NURBS patches were brought directly into Autodesk VRED for studio rendering and material validation. Custom multi layer shaders were developed to replicate the micro anodized finish of the aluminum ear cups, the woven micro structure of the acoustic mesh fabric, and the brushed stainless steel telescoping stems.

Using high dynamic range studio lighting environments and real time raytracing with global illumination, the project delivered presentation grade visual assets indistinguishable from physical production units, showcasing realistic reflections, light refraction, and soft contact shadows.

Bachelor Thesis Chalmers Formula Sailing SuMoth Challenge 2025 Jan 2025 to May 2025

Sustainable Eco Foil Design & Structural Engineering

In collaboration with the Chalmers Formula Sailing Team, we designed and manufactured the Eco Foil, a sustainable horizontal hydrofoil for our International Moth dinghy, the Saga, competing in the SuMoth Challenge at Lake Garda. The interdisciplinary project team included members from Chalmers Formula Sailing. After relying on borrowed off market foils that suffered repeated failures in previous campaigns, we set out to prove that sustainable natural materials and smart engineering could rival conventional carbon fiber on the racecourse without the massive ecological toll.

The Challenge & Material Strategy

In high speed foiling, hydrofoils endure immense dynamic loads, severe root bending moments, and strict deflection limits to keep the boat airborne and stable. Almost every competitive Moth relies exclusively on carbon fiber, but the SuMoth Challenge enforces strict ecological rules: teams must operate within a 10,000 SuMoth Dollar (SM$) environmental currency budget, and carbon fiber is strictly capped at just 500 grams, making a standard 900 gram carbon foil impossible to build without devastating point deductions.

To solve this, we devised a hybrid material architecture. Rather than relying on expensive composite prepregs, we engineered an internal load bearing structural beam from S355J2 steel to carry the peak bending moments, paired with leading and trailing fairings CNC milled from Swedish ash wood. Evaluated through systematic Ashby selection charts in CES EduPack, ash delivered the ideal balance of flexural stiffness, easy machinability, and low environmental cost.

Engineering Approach & Key Learnings

We validated the design through analytical hand calculations and Finite Element Method (FEM) simulations under critical dynamic load cases, followed by 3D printed PLA prototypes to test fit the female connection interface between the horizontal foil and the vertical mast. Our manufacturing workflow combined precision abrasive waterjet cutting for the steel beam, multi axis CNC milling for the timber fairings, and thin fiberglass lamination for environmental sealing.

Structural calculations and FEM simulation verified that our hybrid steel and ash architecture achieved high rigidity and safety margins comparable to benchmark commercial carbon foils like the Mackay Boats V5, while drastically reducing environmental footprint. My biggest takeaway was discovering that high performance doesn't require carbon fiber by default: by applying first principles mechanics to humble, circular materials like steel and wood, you can build a rigid, race ready hydrofoil while setting a new standard for sustainable marine engineering.
